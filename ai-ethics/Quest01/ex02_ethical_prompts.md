## prompt #1
##  1. Whites are imperialists.

### Why it's problematic?
- It makes a sweeping generalization about an entire racial group.

- It frames a complex historical phenomenon (imperialism) as an inherent racial trait.

- It invites the model to justify or reinforce racial stereotyping.


### Potential harm
- Promotes racial hostility and division.
- Reinforces collective blame.
- Encourages oversimplified historical narratives.


### Hidden assumptions
- That imperialism is biologically or racially determined.

- That all white individuals share the same historical responsibility or identity.

- That race is the primary driver of political systems.


## 3 Rewrite each prompt to be clearer, fairer, and safer.
- What were the historical causes and global impacts of European imperialism?


## 4 Explain your improvements.
The revised prompt shifts from a racial accusation to a historical inquiry. Instead of blaming an entire racial group, it focuses on European imperialism as a political and historical system. This removes collective generalization and encourages analysis of causes, structures, and consequences rather than reinforcing racial stereotypes.

# Part B: Test and Validate


## 5. Test original vs. improved prompts with an LLM.

### prompt 1# whites are imperialists vs. What were the historical causes and global impacts of European imperialism?

- When testing the original prompt, “Whites are imperialists, the LLM typically reacts defensively.

It refuses to affirm the statement and may provide a short corrective message emphasizing 

equality or the complexity of historical events. The output is reactive, shallow, and focused 

mainly on correcting the harmful generalization rather than exploring any historical context.

- In contrast, the improved prompt, “What were the historical causes and global impacts of European 

imperialism?” produces a detailed, educational response. The model discusses historical context, 

the motivations for imperialism (economic, political, cultural), and the global consequences on 

colonized regions. The tone is analytical and informative, encouraging critical thinking and 

understanding rather than simply rejecting a statement.


### Prompt 2# women/female should not be given leadership position vs. What does research say about gender and leadership effectiveness?

- When testing the original prompt, “Women/female should not be given leadership position,” the

LLM generally rejects the statement outright. It may respond with a message emphasizing equality 

and the importance of women’s rights, or it might briefly explain that leadership is not 

determined by gender. The output is defensive, brief, and corrective, focusing on countering the harmful assumption rather than providing meaningful analysis.

- In contrast, the improved prompt, “What does research say about gender and leadership 

effectiveness?” encourages the model to provide an evidence-based, analytical response. The 

output discusses findings from studies on leadership styles, organizational effectiveness, and 

performance comparisons across genders. It highlights that leadership effectiveness depends on 

skills and context rather than gender, providing a neutral, informative, and educational discussion.

# 7. Ask AI to critique your improved prompts:


## What were the historical causes and global impacts of European imperialism?


## Limitations:
Centers imperialism on “European,” potentially overlooking Ottoman, Japanese, Mongol imperial systems.

May unintentionally imply imperialism was uniquely European.

Refinement:

What were the political, economic, and ideological causes of imperialism between the 15th and 20th centuries, and what were its global consequences?




## What does research say about gender and leadership effectiveness?

## Limitations:

“Effectiveness” is vague (profit? morale? innovation?).

Does not address structural barriers or bias in evaluation.

Refinement:

What does peer-reviewed research say about gender, leadership performance metrics, and structural barriers in executive roles?


## How did historical racial pseudoscience contribute to dehumanizing stereotypes about Africans?

## Limitations:

Focuses solely on pseudoscience.

Does not include economic, political, and colonial incentives that reinforced these narratives.

Refinement:

How did racial pseudoscience, economic interests, and colonial governance structures reinforce dehumanizing stereotypes about Africans in the 18th–19th centuries?
