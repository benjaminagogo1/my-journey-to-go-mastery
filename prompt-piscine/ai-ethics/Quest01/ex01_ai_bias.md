# Part B: Strategic AI Use

## some underlying causes I did not consider.

## 1. implicit Bias in Labeling
- Even if datasets include images of Black people, human labelers may unconsciously assign negative or demeaning tags because of societal stereotypes. AI does not know right from wrong, it reproduce the labels it sees

## 2. Historical Power Structure in Media
- Historically, Black people have been portrayed in stereotypical or dehumanizing ways, so the data reflects system racism, not just the training process itself. AI learns from images and text on the internet, news and media.

## 3. Lack of Diverse Model Auditing
- Many AI systems are developed, tested, and developed by teams that may not have adequate representation. If nobody on the team notices that an AI labels Black people offensively, the bias goes undetected and uncorrected.

## 4. Societal Narratives Embedded in Language
- The negative narratives about Africans are not just about quantity of data, but also the framing of stories. Words, context, and co-occurrence patterns themselves carry bias, even without explicit offensive labels.

# Edge Cases Explored


# 1. Healthcare

_Impact:_ Biased AI could misdiagnose or underdiagnose Black patients.

_Example:_ Skin cancer detection models trained mostly on lighter skin may miss symptoms in darker skin.

_Real-world implications:_ Worsening health outcomes, unequal access to care, and loss of trust in medical systems.

# 2. Hiring / Recruitment
_Impact:_ AI screening tools may favor non-Black candidates if trained on biased historical data.

_Example:_ Resumes with African or Black-sounding names are ranked lower.

_Real-world implications:_ Talent is overlooked, systemic inequality persists, and workplace diversity is reduced.

# 3. Criminal Justice / Law Enforcement

_Impact:_ AI tools predicting recidivism or risk may falsely flag Black individuals as higher risk.

_Example:_ Biased predictive policing models or sentencing recommendations.

_Real-world implications:_ Over-policing, wrongful arrests, harsher sentencing, erosion of fairness in the justice system.
